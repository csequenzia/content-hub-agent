# Picking and packaging a Reel

These rules come from Greg's validated desktop pipelines. Treat them as the house style.

## The filename is the hook

Clients and Greg name the raw file after the hook they say in the first 3-5 seconds
(for example `What no one tells you about knee pain.mov`). If the filename reads like a
sentence:
- use it, cleaned up, as the on-screen hook, and
- start the clip where that line is spoken, usually at the very start.

If the filename is a camera default (`IMG_4031`, `Front-...` with no words), write the hook yourself.

## Choosing the section

- One continuous section, 30-60 seconds. Shorter and complete beats longer and padded.
- The first sentence must work as a scroll-stopper on its own: a surprising claim, a
  mistake the viewer is making, a strong opinion, or a direct "you" statement.
- It must end on a finished thought, ideally the payoff or the call to action. Never
  end mid-list or on "and so...".
- Skip warm-up talk ("so today I want to talk about..."), bloopers, restarts and
  repeated takes. If the speaker restarts a line, use the last clean take.
- Healthcare words Whisper gets wrong: patience -> patients, dietician -> dietitian,
  cloud -> Claude. Read the transcript with that in mind.

## On-screen hook

- Under 8 words. Plain words, ALL CAPS on screen (the renderer does the caps).
- Says what the clip delivers. No promises the clip does not keep.

## Post caption

- 2-5 short lines in the client's voice from the brand kit.
- First line restates the hook in different words.
- End with the client's CTA from the brand kit if there is one (Greg's style is
  `COMMENT "KEYWORD" BELOW`).
- 3-6 hashtags, specific to the profession and the topic, no generic #fyp spam.

## Look (handled by the renderer, listed so you know)

1080x1920, 3-word ALL-CAPS captions low on screen, hook pill at the top for the first
seconds, client logo top-right, brand colors from the kit.
